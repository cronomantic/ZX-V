The firmware

Each Pico needs its own program, called its firmware. It comes as a .uf2 file that you copy onto the Pico like a file onto a USB stick: no special software is needed.

Pico File What it contains
CPU Pico zxv_cpu.uf2 The machine: the BASIC, the services (USB, SD card, clock) and the ROM: drive with the built-in examples
ULA Pico zxv_ula_link.uf2 The picture and the sound, received from the CPU Pico

Be careful with the ULA file: it is zxv_ula_link.uf2, not zxv_ula.uf2. The second one is a test that shows some demos on its own, without the CPU Pico.

Getting the files#

  1. Go to the Releases page of the project on GitHub (https://github.com/cronomantic/ZX-V/releases).
  2. In the latest version, download zxv-uf2.zip and unzip it. It contains all the .uf2 files: you need zxv_cpu.uf2 and zxv_ula_link.uf2. The others are tests (see Test programs).

Putting the firmware on a Pico#

  1. Hold down the BOOTSEL button on the Pico (the only button on it) and, while holding it, connect the Pico's micro-USB to your computer. Then release the button.
  2. A drive called RP2350 appears on the computer.
  3. Copy the .uf2 file onto that drive (drag and drop). When the copy finishes, the Pico restarts on its own with the new firmware and the drive disappears.

Do it once for each Pico, with its own file.

  • Before soldering. On the ZX-V board it is easiest to flash each Pico before soldering it, and to label it (CPU or ULA): the two look identical. Remember: the ULA Pico goes on the left and the CPU Pico on the right, seen from the front.
  • Once on the board. The micro-USB connector and the BOOTSEL button are still reachable. Plug the Pico into the computer with the board switched on: the board's diodes keep the two supplies apart. With the board off, a single Pico on USB would feed the other one through the link pins, and the RESET they share could hold it in reset.
  • On a protoboard where your 5 V supply is connected to a Pico's VBUS pin (pin 40), disconnect that supply first, or its 5 V would reach your computer through the USB cable (On a protoboard).

Updating#

Download the new zxv-uf2.zip and repeat the steps above for both Picos. Always use the two files from the same version: if the CPU and the ULA have different versions of their link, the CPU says so in its report (see below), and the picture may not appear.

Without pressing BOOTSEL. If you have picotool installed (it comes with the Raspberry Pi Pico extension of VS Code), a Pico that is already running a ZX-V firmware can be updated through its USB cable, without touching BOOTSEL:

picotool load -f -x zxv_cpu.uf2

-f restarts the Pico in loading mode and -x starts the new firmware when it has finished. If the Pico has hung, or has never had ZX-V firmware, use BOOTSEL.

The serial port and the report#

The CPU Pico's micro-USB is also a serial port. Open it with any terminal program (for example the Serial Monitor of VS Code, PuTTY on Windows, or screen /dev/ttyACM0 on Linux; the speed does not matter). Two things happen there:

  • What you type in the terminal reaches ZX-V as if typed on its keyboard. Handy to try the machine before you have a USB keyboard.
  • Every 5 seconds the CPU Pico writes a report, in the machine's language (the one LANGUAGE chooses), about the link, the sound, the SD card, the USB devices, the clock and the ULA. It is the best place to look when something does not work.

The same messages also come out of the board's UART header (J5) at 921600 baud. The ULA Pico writes its own messages on its micro-USB.

If there is no picture or no sound#

No picture#

Look at the red link LED (ENLACE on the board) when you switch on.

  • It never blinks. The ULA Pico is not running its firmware: check that it has zxv_ula_link.uf2 and that it gets power.
  • It blinks, then stays off. The ULA works but receives nothing from the CPU Pico. Check that the CPU Pico has zxv_cpu.uf2 (and the ULA has not been given the CPU's file by mistake). On a protoboard, check the link wiring.
  • It blinks, then stays on. Images arrive correctly: the problem is between the ULA and the screen. Try the safe mode (below), another HDMI input, or a shorter cable without adapters. On the board, the report on the serial port says whether the screen is detected (screen: connected): if it stays unknown with the screen plugged in and switched on, look at the HDMI cable and connector.
  • It keeps blinking. Images arrive, but some with errors. On a protoboard, shorten the link wires, add the 33 Ω resistors and more ground wires.

The number of blinks at start-up is the video mode plus one (once for mode 0, twice for mode 1).

The safe mode. Hold MODE down (MODO on the board) while switching on: the ULA starts in DVI, which every screen should accept. This lasts only until the next restart. To keep a mode, press MODE briefly with the machine running: each short press switches between HDMI and DVI, saves it and restarts the ULA. If your screen only shows the safe mode, press MODE until the link LED blinks twice at start-up: that is mode 1, the same as the safe mode, and it will be kept.

Mode Output Sound on the screen
0 HDMI 640 × 480, 60 Hz (default) Yes
1 DVI 640 × 480, 60 Hz (safe mode) No

You see moving demos instead of the BASIC (a scrolling game, coloured stripes, circles): the ULA Pico has zxv_ula.uf2, the test. Put zxv_ula_link.uf2 on it.

Dots that flicker over a recognisable picture are transmission errors between the ULA and the screen. On a protoboard, look for a loose wire and keep the HDMI wires short and in pairs. Some TVs also need 5 V on pin 18 of the HDMI connector (On a protoboard).

No sound#

  • In mode 1 (DVI) the screen has no sound. Switch to mode 0 with MODE, or use the audio jack.
  • The audio jack is line level: it needs powered speakers, an amplifier or a TV's audio input. Headphones need an amplifier.
  • Try a sound from the BASIC: BEEP 1,0 or PLAY "cdefgab".
  • On a protoboard, the sound travels on its own wire, from GP20 of the CPU Pico (pin 26) to GP21 of the ULA Pico (pin 27). Without it there is a picture but no sound.
  • A little hiss on the audio jack is normal.

Other things#

  • The keyboard does nothing. Look at the report on the serial port: the USB: part says how many keyboards, mice and gamepads it sees. Try without a hub, or with a powered hub.
  • The SD card does not work. It must be FAT, FAT32 or exFAT. The report says no card until the first access (for example CAT), or if the card does not answer. On the board the card is mounted as soon as it goes in.
  • The report says no ULA: timer: the CPU Pico is not receiving the ULA's frame signal. On a protoboard, check the wire between the two GP27 pins (pin 32).
  • The report says no back channel: the wire from GP20 of the ULA (pin 26) to GP18 of the CPU (pin 24) is missing. Everything works without it, except that programs are always told the output is HDMI.

The diagnostic page#

A long press of MODE (between 1 and 5 seconds) or Ctrl+Alt+F12 on the keyboard shows a blue band across the middle of the screen with the state of both Picos, updated every second: at the top the ULA (its mode, the images per second, link errors, the CRC of the palettes it receives, the sound); at the bottom the CPU (link, sound, SD card, USB, clock). If the CPU's part does not arrive within 2.5 seconds, the page says so. Do the same again to hide it. It is in the machine's language: the CPU sends it to the ULA with every frame. The ULA Pico's own messages on its micro-USB follow it too, once the link is working.

Test programs#

zxv-uf2.zip also contains test programs. Two are useful when building:

File On What it does
zxv_ula.uf2 One Pico 2 alone The ULA's picture and sound with some demos, without the CPU Pico. Proves the HDMI output and the audio jack. The MODE button works the same
zxv_hdmi.uf2 One Pico 2 alone Colour bars and a test tone on each side (left, right, centre) over HDMI. zxv_dvi.uf2 is the same in DVI, without sound

The rest (zxv_linktx, zxv_bench, zxv_bench_ram, zxv_isatest) are for the firmware's developers. Remember to put the right firmware back afterwards.
