Building the hardware

ZX-V is not only an emulator: you can build the real machine and plug it into a TV. It is made from two Raspberry Pi Pico 2 boards, a handful of connectors and a few dozen small parts. You do not need to write any code: the software for both Picos comes ready to copy onto them.

Status. The firmware for both Picos is written, and each Pico has been tested on its own: the HDMI picture and sound on a single Pico 2, and the CPU, which runs programs exactly like the emulator. The two Picos have not yet been tested working together, so the complete machine has not been tested, neither on the PCB nor on a protoboard. The printed circuit board (PCB) is fully designed, but it has not been manufactured or tested yet. If you build one now, you will be among the first to try it: read The board carefully and expect that something may need fixing.

What you need#

  • Two Raspberry Pi Pico 2 boards. They must be the Pico 2 (with the RP2350 chip): the original Raspberry Pi Pico does not run the firmware.
  • The board, ordered assembled from JLCPCB, and the few parts you solder yourself (The board). Or, instead, a protoboard or breadboard and the parts in On a protoboard.
  • The firmware: two .uf2 files, one for each Pico (The firmware).
  • A screen with an HDMI input (a TV or a monitor) and an HDMI cable.
  • A USB keyboard. Optionally a USB mouse, one or two USB gamepads and a USB hub to plug them all in.
  • A microSD card, to save your programs and load others.
  • A USB-C phone charger of 5 V that can give 2 A, with its cable.
  • Optionally, powered speakers for the audio output, and a CR2032 coin cell so the clock keeps the date and time.
  • A soldering iron, solder, and a computer with a micro-USB cable to put the firmware on the Picos.

Both run the same firmware and behave the same.

What each Pico does#

The two Picos split the work like the chips in the original Spectrum. The CPU Pico is the computer itself: it runs the BASIC and your programs, and looks after the keyboard, mouse and gamepads (through the USB-A port), the SD card and the clock. The ULA Pico makes the picture and the sound: it receives each finished image from the CPU Pico, sends it to the screen over HDMI, and plays the sound both on the HDMI and on the audio jack. It also carries the video mode button and the link LED. They talk over an 8-bit bus and a few extra wires. For your programs there is only one machine: they cannot tell that there are two chips.

Connecting it#

The screen (HDMI)#

Plug any HDMI TV or monitor into the HDMI connector. The picture is always 640 × 480 at 60 Hz, a mode every HDMI screen must accept, and the ULA Pico has two output modes:

Mode Output Sound on the screen The link LED blinks at start-up
0 HDMI (the default) Yes Once
1 DVI (the safe mode) No Twice
  • A short press of the MODE button switches to the other mode. The ULA Pico restarts with it (the BASIC carries on) and remembers it when you switch off.
  • Holding MODE down while you switch on starts in the safe mode, just that once. Every screen should accept it, including old DVI monitors that reject HDMI.
  • Programs cannot change the mode, only read it.

Sound#

The sound comes out on the HDMI (in mode 0) and, always, on the 3.5 mm stereo jack (labelled AUDIO). The jack gives a line-level signal: connect powered speakers, an amplifier or your TV's audio input. Headphones need an amplifier.

Keyboard, mouse and gamepads#

Plug them into the USB-A port at the front. For more than one device, use a USB hub (a powered hub is best). ZX-V understands ordinary USB keyboards, mice and most gamepads.

  • Ctrl+Alt+F1, F2 and F3 choose the keyboard layout: Spanish, British or US. The machine remembers it.
  • Ctrl+Alt+Del is the emergency reset: it takes you back to the BASIC even if a program has hung, and keeps your BASIC program.
  • Ctrl+Alt+F12 shows or hides the diagnostic page (see The firmware).

The machine explains how the keyboard, mouse and gamepads are seen by programs.

The SD card#

Insert a microSD card in the slot on the right-hand side. It can be formatted FAT, FAT32 or exFAT (cards come formatted like this), of any size. You can take it out and put it back with the machine on. On the board the machine notices at once: a card that goes in is ready, and a file that was open when it came out can only be closed (what was already written to it is kept). Close your files before taking the card out. The green SD LED lights while the machine reads or writes the card.

The built-in examples are not on the card: they are in the ROM: drive, which comes inside the CPU Pico's firmware.

Power#

Plug a USB-C charger into the 5 V connector at the back, and slide the power switch (at the front, on the left) to the right, to ON; to the left is OFF. A 2 A charger is plenty: the whole machine uses under 1 A, including the USB devices. The USB-C port only takes power; it does not carry data.

The clock and the language#

The board has a real-time clock with a CR2032 coin cell. It keeps the date and time (used to date the files you save), the machine's language and the keyboard layout while the power is off. Set the time from the BASIC with CLOCK, for example CLOCK 2026,10,20,18,30 (year, month, day, hour and minutes).

The machine starts in English. Type LANGUAGE "es" to switch to Spanish; with the coin cell in place it is remembered.
